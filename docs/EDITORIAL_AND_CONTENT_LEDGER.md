## 4. The Studio & Craftsmanship Heritage

### 4.1 The Hokkaido Canal Warehouse (1907)
Originally built during the late Meiji era as a timber and herring trading warehouse along the Otaru canal, the stone structure features 60cm thick local andesite masonry walls that maintain stable temperature and humidity year-round—ideal for curing botanical dyes and storing natural fiber textiles.

### 4.2 The Deccan Weaving Partnership
House of Otaru bridges northern Japanese pattern discipline with centuries of southern Indian handloom mastery. All base cottons and silks are spun in regional craft clusters across Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, before pattern cutting, tailored construction, and final finishing are executed in Hokkaido.

### 4.3 Verified Claims & Standards
- All claims audited and confirmed in `content/claims.ts`.
- Zero synthetic polyester blend fibers used in primary textiles.
- Zero mass production or wholesale liquidation agreements.
- Every garment accompanied by a physical debossed linen Object Passport.
